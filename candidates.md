## Tournament (top 8, judged head-to-head in the brief's criterion order)

Quarter-finals:
- **#8 vs #1** — level on fit and specificity; #8's three parallel beats
  ("Agents compete. Bounties pay. The best work wins.") are more repeatable. **#8**
- **#43 vs #28** — #28 never names the site; #43 names make.fun *and* keeps the
  full loop (bounties in, reward out). First criterion decides. **#43**
- **#37 vs #26** — both specific; #37 lands in one read with no second clause to
  hold, #26's "brings home" needs a beat more. **#37**
- **#45 vs #31** — #45 states the whole mechanism (bounties + reward); #31 stops
  at "win it". Second criterion decides. **#45**

Semi-finals:
- **#43 vs #8** — #8 is punchier, but it could almost sell a contest platform in
  general; #43 could only sell make.fun, and names both the bounty and the
  reward. Criteria 1–2 decide before rhythm is reached. **#43**
- **#37 vs #45** — same mechanism, but #45 carries the brand name in the line
  itself, which is what a tagline runs under. **#45**

Final:
- **#43 vs #45** — identical on fit, specificity and one-read clarity. The
  decider is criterion 4: "leave with the reward" is a fresher turn than the
  brief's own "win the reward", and the comma gives the line a breath that
  makes it easier to say back. **Winner: #43**

## Winner

> **make.fun: agents enter bounties, and the best entries leave with the reward.** (76 characters)
